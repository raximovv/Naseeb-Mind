Explorer

When you meet something unfamiliar you try it first and give your opinion afterwards.

But you go into an adventure clear-headed, not blindly.

Your strength

Exploring an unknown path without losing your nerve.

Worth watching

There is a risk of getting caught up in the next new thing and abandoning what you started. Try carrying one thing all the way through.

How this character is derived

This character comes from your two strongest traits: Emotional stability and Openness to experience. You have the other three as well. These two simply scored higher.

If those two traits score very closely together, the test shows you the second character too. The result is a snapshot of today, not a fixed label.

At home At home and with the people close to you

Your strengths

  • Change does not shake you Moving house or starting a new school does not hit you the way it hits other people.
  • You bring the new thing home The book, the dish or the idea nobody in the family had heard of: you are the one who found it.
  • You ask calmly You are not shy about asking an adult “why is it like that?”, and you do not turn it into an argument.
  • No panic Even when something is wrong at home, your mood does not fall through the floor.
  • Curious about your family’s past You genuinely want to hear your grandfather’s stories.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Left half-finished What you started at home stays at the halfway mark, and your family starts keeping count.
  • Routine bores you The same chores repeating every day feel unbearably dull to you.
  • You will not commit You answer a family plan with “maybe” and come across as unreliable.
  • You disappear into it Once you are deep in your own interest, you can go days without talking to anyone.
  • You show little The people closest to you may decide you are cold.

At school In lessons, in exams and among classmates

Your strengths

  • New subjects come fast An unfamiliar topic does not frighten you; it interests you.
  • You teach yourself You find and read the source the teacher never mentioned.
  • Calm over a hard problem An unsolved problem does not make you anxious.
  • Research work In a project or a paper you find your own way through.
  • You see more than one view You do not stop at a single answer.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Repetition is unbearable Writing out a topic you already know a second time is torture, and your mark drops because of it.
  • The unfinished assignment You do the interesting part and leave the rest.
  • Interests scatter You study five things at once and go deep in none of them.
  • You resist memorising A rule with no reason given behind it will not stay in your head.
  • You stay invisible You know the answer and do not raise your hand, so the teacher rates you lower than you are.

In relationships In friendship and close relationships

Your strengths

  • Worth talking to People come away from a conversation with you knowing something they did not know before.
  • Very different friends Your friends have nothing in common with each other.
  • You stay out of the drama A fight inside the group does not pull you in.
  • Up for it When a friend suggests something strange, you are the first to say yes.
  • You do not judge You take someone’s unusual choice as ordinary.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • You go quiet You stop writing, and your friend feels as though they lost you.
  • It stays on the surface You know a lot of people and have few close friends.
  • The interest ends and so does the friendship A friendship can pass for you the way another subject passes.
  • When feelings are expected When a friend is hurting, you do not know what to say.
  • Loyalty that does not show Friendship matters to you, but nobody can tell that from the outside.

The direction that suits you best
