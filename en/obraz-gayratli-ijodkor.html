Creator

Your ideas never run out, and you like telling people about them.

Your strength

Making a new idea catch on.

Worth watching

How this character is derived

This character comes from your two strongest traits: Extraversion and Openness to experience. You have the other three as well. These two simply scored higher.

If those two traits score very closely together, the test shows you the second character too. The result is a snapshot of today, not a fixed label.

At home At home and with the people close to you

Your strengths

  • You bring the house to life When you are home, it is never quiet.
  • You come up with the plan The idea for what to do at the weekend usually starts with you.
  • You tell the story You can make an ordinary day sound funny.
  • You play with the little ones Your younger siblings are never bored with you.
  • You are open You do not hide what happened to you from your family.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Unfinished projects The things you started pile up in the corner of the room.
  • Forgotten errands Something interesting comes up and what you were asked to do slips your mind.
  • Cutting in When you get excited you talk over people before they finish.
  • Hard to sit still at home Quiet family evenings feel dull to you.
  • You and tidiness Your room is always a mess, and it starts arguments.

At school In lessons, in exams and among classmates

Your strengths

  • Presenting You feel free in front of a room.
  • Open-ended work When the topic is yours to choose, your work is the one people remember.
  • In a discussion Trading ideas is enjoyable for you, not stressful.
  • Waking the class up One question from you brings a whole lesson to life.
  • Getting others interested You can pull your classmates into a project.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Deadlines Plenty of ideas, not many finished pieces of work.
  • Drifting off In class your mind wanders somewhere else.
  • Repetitive practice Solving the same kind of problem ten times is painful for you.
  • A messy notebook Later you cannot find what you wrote down yourself.
  • Marks below what you know You know the material, but work you never handed in pulls the result down.

In relationships In friendship and close relationships

Your strengths

  • The middle of the group The mood lifts when you arrive.
  • You connect people You introduce friends from different circles to each other.
  • Never dull No two days with you are the same.
  • You get people going You remind a friend of the plan they gave up on.
  • You warm up fast You can find common ground with a stranger in a day.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Many friends, few close ones Everyone knows you; not many know you properly.
  • Forgotten arrangements You agree to meet and then it goes out of your head.
  • Taking over the conversation You enjoy talking so much that there is no room left for listening.
  • Cooling off quickly A new group appears and the old friends are left to one side.
  • Avoiding being alone To avoid being on your own you will join a group that does not suit you.

The direction that suits you best

  • Design and visual workMain direction

    Your ideas do not run out, and you are not afraid to show them to people.

Alongside it, you can pick one of these three directions

  • Game designExtra

    You turn story, art and rules into one world, and other people get to play in it.

  • Museum and exhibition curatingExtra

    Turning an old object into a story people stop to look at is creative work.

  • Gastronomy and culinary artsExtra

    Food is a stage too: inventing a new taste and presenting it to people suits you.
