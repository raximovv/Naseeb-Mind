Heart of the Group

In a room you have walked into, conversation starts on its own.

You notice the person left on the edge of things, and you call them over.

Your strength

Bringing people together.

Worth watching

Trying to be on good terms with everyone, you may never say what you actually think. Your view matters too.

How this character is derived

This character comes from your two strongest traits: Extraversion and Agreeableness. You have the other three as well. These two simply scored higher.

If those two traits score very closely together, the test shows you the second character too. The result is a snapshot of today, not a fixed label.

At home At home and with the people close to you

Your strengths

  • Warmth in the house In the room you are in, everyone relaxes.
  • Nobody is left out The relative sitting silently is the one you pull into the conversation.
  • You keep in touch You are the one who calls the distant relatives.
  • Hospitality Someone who comes to your house never feels like an outsider.
  • You lift the mood You can make a heavy day at home lighter.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • You go around the argument Instead of naming a disagreement out loud, you get past it with a joke.
  • You keep your own view quiet To hold the peace you give up what you actually wanted.
  • Good with everyone When two relatives fall out, you tell both of them they are right.
  • More often out than in When the house goes quiet, you leave for your friends.
  • You hide being tired Even on a day you feel low, you look cheerful.

At school In lessons, in exams and among classmates

Your strengths

  • You tie the class together Between the groups in your class, you are the bridge.
  • You take in the new student The new pupil who arrives in class is met by you first.
  • Team spirit In group work you make sure everyone takes part.
  • Warm with teachers A lesson turns open and easy when you are in it.
  • Active at events A class event without you is hard to picture.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Talking beats the lesson You get into a conversation and the topic goes past you.
  • You avoid the disagreement You leave even a wrong answer uncorrected so that nobody feels hurt.
  • You do not defend your work Even when a mark is unfair, you say nothing.
  • Attention scatters With people around, the lesson drops to second place.
  • Studying alone is hard Sitting on your own and going over it again is heavy work for you.

In relationships In friendship and close relationships

Your strengths

  • A lot of friends In very different circles you are one of their own.
  • You see the one on the edge You notice the person who has gone quiet in the group and call them over.
  • You share the joy You are happier about your friend’s win than about your own.
  • You bring people back together Two friends who fell out end up at the same table again because of you.
  • You remember Birthdays stay in your head.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Not enough of you to go round You have many friends, and none of them gets enough of your time.
  • You cannot say no Even worn out, you do not turn down the invitation.
  • Quick to be hurt An ordinary joke lands on you personally.
  • You smother the conflict Rather than talk the problem through you close it, and it comes back.
  • You change to fit in In each circle you are slightly different, and you lose track of yourself.

The direction that suits you best

  • Teaching and educationMain direction

    Holding a class together and seeing every child in it: that is your strength.

Alongside it, you can pick one of these three directions

  • Working with children in hospitalExtra

    A rare job helping children in long treatment get past their fear through play and conversation.

  • Eco-tourism guidingExtra

    You turn a group of strangers into friends in a day, and nature is the best setting for it.

  • Inclusive and Paralympic sports coachingExtra

    Making every player feel part of the team comes naturally to you.
