Kind Soul

New ideas and care for people live side by side in you.

When you see a problem, you think about the people first.

Your strength

Finding an answer that is both useful and humane.

Worth watching

You take on other people’s problems as your own. You do not have to solve everything alone.

How this character is derived

This character comes from your two strongest traits: Openness to experience and Agreeableness. You have the other three as well. These two simply scored higher.

If those two traits score very closely together, the test shows you the second character too. The result is a snapshot of today, not a fixed label.

At home At home and with the people close to you

Your strengths

  • You feel the mood You know your mother is tired before she says so.
  • Care with imagination Your gift is not bought, it is thought up.
  • You do not look for who is to blame When something goes wrong at home, you do not turn it into a question of whose fault it was.
  • You listen to the other view With adults you try to understand rather than argue.
  • You make the house beautiful The room and the table get decorated your own way.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • You carry the family’s trouble A problem at home weighs on you more than on anyone else.
  • The practical side passes you by Money, timetables and plans slip past your attention.
  • You avoid the collision Even when you disagree, you do not say it out loud.
  • You idealise You picture your family as flawless, so a flaw in it hits you hard.
  • You shut yourself in When you are hurt, you go to your room.

At school In lessons, in exams and among classmates

Your strengths

  • The creative project When the assignment is open, your work is the one people remember.
  • You help the one who is struggling You explain it to the classmate who did not understand, and nobody else finds out.
  • The fairness question “Is this fair?” is the question you are the one to ask.
  • No fight over marks You do not compete for grades, and the class is calmer for it.
  • An eye for how things look Your notebook, your slides and your drawings come out beautiful.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • Criticism cuts deep A small remark from a teacher can spoil your mood for days.
  • Disorder Deadlines and timetables get tangled for you.
  • You drop the subject you dislike On a subject that does not interest you, you spend no effort at all.
  • You underrate yourself If maths is hard for you, you write off your creative gift along with it.
  • You stay quiet You have a good idea and you do not say it in front of the class.

In relationships In friendship and close relationships

Your strengths

  • The deep conversation Small talk does not hold you, so you ask about the thing that matters.
  • You take people as they are A friend tells you even the thing they are most ashamed of.
  • You pick up on it You hear the mood in someone’s voice.
  • A friendship of its own kind Your friendship does not look like anybody else’s.
  • You forgive You do not carry a mistake around for long.

Worth keeping an eye on

  • You take on the hurt Your friend’s problem torments you more than it torments them.
  • You idealise people You see someone as flawless, and when the truth shows, the disappointment is heavy.
  • You drift away without saying When you are hurt you do not explain; you quietly disappear.
  • No boundary For a friend you give your time away completely.
  • You avoid the conflict Rather than say what the problem is, you let the friendship cool.
